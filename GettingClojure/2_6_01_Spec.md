
## CHAPTER 15 Spec

One thing you may have noticed about Clojure programs—and Clojure programmers—is that they rarely talk about data types. Oh, sure, we have strings and symbols and maps and vectors along with various record types, but what’s important in Clojure is less the data type than the data shape.

Thus we tend not to think of our data as an `AggregateBookCollection`, which contains instances of `BookEntities`, each one having instances of `Author` and `Title`.  Instead we look at it as a vector of maps, where each map has `:title`, `:author`, and `:copies` keys.

Since Clojure is more concerned with the shape of data than its type, Clojure programmers spend a fair amount of time verifying that the value they’ve just been handed has the right shape. "Is this really a vector of maps where each map has a `:title` key?" is a common sort of question to ask of a value passed to your function. So in this chapter we’re going to look at a relatively recent addition to the Clojure ecosystem: `clojure.spec`, a library that enables you to validate the shape of your values.
